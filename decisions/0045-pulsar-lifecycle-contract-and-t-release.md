## Allocation, replacement and destruction

Use owned allocation identity and typed views for pointer-backed state. Observation views carry an owner and an offset into complete records, independent of nobs. They must support S's disjoint, shared and both overlapping-buffer histories. Handles remain meaningful when Rust values move; a released handle cannot silently name a later allocation. A small owned context with typed handles is appropriate; do not add a general allocator/plugin framework.

Initialization performs the source writes in their actual order: nobs reset; observation allocation without element initialization; full covariance allocation; nonparameter assignments; parameter allocation and label/default writes; final constraint-pointer writes. Preserve interleaved writes such as dm_series_type in the label section. Reuse O's established labels and capacities rather than maintaining a second initializer. allocateMemory resets link counts and replaces the dynamic buffers, while **retaining each embedded linkTo/linkFrom cell and its knownness**. New prefit/prefitErr buffers are unwritten.

Repeated initialization/allocation must make overwritten allocations available to callers that retain them. A retained owner refers to the original allocation, not a reconstructed deep copy. Use explicit owner handles or a returned retired-allocation bundle, with one documented ownership path in the implementation. Retained allocations stay inspectable until their owner releases them. Source-omitted embedded fields retain their values. Source-null assignments, including ToAextraCovar and constraint_special, do not imply that their former allocations were freed.

Rust need not reproduce unreachable C leaks. Dropping an unretained owned allocation or the whole context reclaims it, consistently with O's reviewed ownership boundary. Document this alongside replacement/drop code. Explicit retained-owner histories must still match S. Do not promise arbitrary foreign-pointer or shallow-C-struct ABI compatibility.

destroyOne follows the source's logical order: optional observation release, covariance release, all MAX_PARAMS constraint_special entries independently of nconstraints, then destroyMemory. It does not free ToAextraCovar or clear the source fields. Released logical identities may remain for diagnostics, but cannot be dereferenced. Preserve destroyMemory's source loop/read/release order; reclaiming otherwise unreachable owned label tails on Drop remains O's explicit departure. S establishes successful destruction and inspected free order, not a native measurement of each free. Rust lifetime tests must identify what they actually observe.

Invalid/null covariance, interior/foreign owner release, double release, unwritten reads and source-invalid capacity/arithmetic paths return explicit operation-local safe boundaries. Keep completed writes and the diagnostic prefix; do not silently reset the input or permit it to produce successful scientific output after a terminal boundary.
